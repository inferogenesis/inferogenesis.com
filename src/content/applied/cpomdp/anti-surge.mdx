## The result

Based on the work by [Sathish, Khader, Sayma and Shah](https://doi.org/10.1115/GT2024-124905) I took the opportunity to point cpomdp at a real world industrial application. Doing some quick research and then math derivations revealed that the R(x) paper would yet again prove useful in helping us understand the mechanics of how to build one of these sensors in the linear Gaussian regime without being reduced to a Kalman filter.

Previous work by Sathish et al., briefly summarised, looked at designing a natural gas centrifugal compressor that is prone to surge and simulating it in an Aspen HYSYS dynamic model, with active inference put forward as an alternative to PID and model predictive control when the conditions get noisy and uncertain.

What they don't get into is the flow meter. They make the case that active inference copes well with noisy and uncertain conditions, and the uncertainty they focus on is the gas arriving at the compressor, with suction conditions that shift over a reservoir's life. The noise in the flow reading itself, and how that noise changes as the flow moves, doesn't come up. That turns out to matter. An active inference agent is curious, and it will happily drift toward wherever its meter reads best, even when that's past the surge line.

Here I wanted to take cpomdp and fill in the gaps. Setting a cpomdp agent to hold a centrifugal compressor's flow at a 5% margin
above the surge line, in percent of design flow, I then changed its flow meter and
nothing else. Each meter is 0.2% accurate at design flow. Only the shape of its error
changes.

| Flow meter | First step | Settles at |
| --- | --- | --- |
| DP, with its noise frozen at the operating point | +5.03% | +5.00% |
| Orifice plate or venturi (differential pressure) | +12.30% | +7.14% |
| Coriolis | −4.98% | −0.38% |

The setpoint is +5%. A negative margin is past the surge line. "Settles at" is cpomdp's
own `Agent` run in closed loop, with no noise.

The DP agent stands off on the safe side. The Coriolis agent aims past the surge line on
its first step. It settles there too.

## Why the meter decides

The agent's objective has an information term. It rewards a reading by how much the
reading would teach, and quiet(low noise) readings teach most. So the agent drifts toward the flows
its own meter reads best. Not a cautionary value as stated by Sathish et al.

A DP element reads flow through a square root, and its transmitter error is roughly flat
in pressure. Its flow noise is worst at low flow, near surge. A Coriolis meter's error is
a fixed share of the reading so its noise is lowest at low flow meaning The sign flips. The term
that holds the DP agent off surge pulls the Coriolis agent in. The
[cpomdp docs](https://cpomdp.inferogenesis.com/guides/surge_margin/) derive both noise
models and a closed-form law for where the agent stands.

## If you run compressors

- **The information term is not a safety factor.** With a DP meter it looks like
  caution. It is appetite for information, and here it happens to point away from surge.
- **Surge protection belongs in the setpoint or a hard limit.** Real surge margin prices
  the cost of being wrong. Curiosity cannot stand in for that.
- **Coordinates decide whether the term does any work.** Many anti-surge controllers
  work in reduced ΔP coordinates. A linear-Gaussian model with additive control, written
  there, has constant noise. Its information term then takes the same value for every
  action, and the agent becomes a plain controller.
- **The meter's error model is part of the controller.** An active-inference controller
  needs it. Above all it needs the sign of its slope.

## What this is not

A showcase on an illustrative compressor. It has no surge dynamics and no compressor map,
and its operating point describes no real machine.

- The DP noise model comes from ISO 5167, which excludes pulsating flow. Surge is
  pulsating flow. The model describes the meter on the approach to surge.
- The Coriolis meter has no zero-stability term. A real one has a fixed error near zero
  flow, which would weaken its pull toward low flow.
- It is not a controller benchmark. The baseline is the same agent with frozen noise.
- The setpoint's weight is a policy choice and sets the size of every offset above. The
  direction is the result.

The application follows Sathish, Khader, Sayma and Shah, who propose active inference
for anti-surge control in
[GT2024-124905](https://doi.org/10.1115/GT2024-124905) (ASME Turbo Expo 2024). The
modelling here is mine.
