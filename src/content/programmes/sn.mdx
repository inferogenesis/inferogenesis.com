title: Seam Networks
premise:
  title: A string of thought experiments
  text: >-
    Seam Networks is a string of thought experiments of mine. They grew out of cpomdp's
    work on the [p* programme](/programmes/p-star/) and its
    [harness](https://github.com/inferogenesis/cpomdp/blob/3cb17dc/src/cpomdp/harness.py),
    which keeps a [[world]] and its [[agent|agents]] as separate objects with no reference
    between them. Nothing here has run. The first question is whether the structure is
    worth exploring at all, and whether cpomdp can tell.
question: 'Active inference agents that meet only through a shared world: does that structure make sense to explore, and can cpomdp determine whether it does?'
scope: >-
  Every [[agent]] is the same kind of object, with its own [[factor graph]] and its own
  [[expected free energy]]. Its model changes by rung. Agents meet only through a
  [[world]] that advances once per [[tick]]. That join, the [[seam]], is the only one the
  ladder uses. It is a construction. Two narrower questions test it. What does the join
  keep that a [[shared latent]] does not? When the world between two cells changes, does
  the receiving cell's model have to change? The registered targets below make both
  precise. The ladder is ordered by [[inference dimensionality]]. It is not a phylogeny. A
  [[Pearl blanket|Markov blanket]] here is Pearl's, read off a stated graph. No result will
  claim a [[Friston blanket]].

  - id: BN-3b
    claim: The same two cells on DirectChannel and on DiffusionField2D give the same qualitative ordering of synchrony and [[inference gap]], with a slower rise under the field.
  - id: BN-5a
    claim: The [[CI test]] passes at the graph's topological cut and at the field's spatial cut. The two cuts disagree on membership.
  - id: BN-5b
    claim: The boundary CI holds with the coordinator inside the region or in a sibling. The agency degree is larger for sibling placement at every coupling strength tested.
  - id: T3
    claim: Two cells sharing an [[equality node]] with two preference vectors do not define one variational problem. Without identical preferences or declared arbitration, the fixed point depends on update order.
  - id: T7
    claim: For [[LG]] cells with linear policies, the region's medium carries an affine function of the posterior means. The mean error is bounded. Covariances do not cross the interface.
  - id: T9
    claim: With the coordinator in a sibling region, the boundary CI still holds. The agency degree there exceeds the in-region value, monotone in coupling strength.
  - id: T10
    claim: A graph cut is reported only if its CI statistic beats a null of random cuts matched on size and crossing-edge count.

terms:
  - term: "agent"
    definition: "A cell modelled as an active inference agent. It has its own factor graph and chooses its actions by its own expected free energy. Every agent is the same kind of object. Its model changes by rung."
  - term: "world"
    definition: "Everything outside the agents. It holds state, takes each agent's action, advances once per tick and hands each agent its observation."
  - term: "tick"
    definition: "One step of the shared clock. The world advances once per tick."
  - term: "seam"
    definition: "Where an agent meets the world. Actions leave across it and observations arrive across it. No reference runs from one agent to another, so every coupling passes through the world."
  - term: "world object"
    definition: "The code that plays the world at one rung, such as a well-mixed medium or a synaptic cleft. The names in code font are planned. None ships yet."
  - term: "factor graph"
    definition: "A drawing of a probabilistic model as nodes and edges. Each agent's model is one, and its inference runs as messages passed along the edges."
  - term: "expected free energy"
    definition: "The score an active inference agent minimises when it picks an action. It weighs reaching the observations it prefers against resolving its uncertainty about the world."
  - term: "preferences"
    definition: "The observations an agent would rather see. They sit inside its expected free energy, so two agents with their own preferences have two separate scores."
  - term: "shared latent"
    definition: "A hidden variable that sits in two agents' factor graphs at once. It couples them without going through the world."
  - term: "equality node"
    definition: "A factor-graph node that forces the variables on its edges to be equal. Two graphs joined at one become a single graph."
  - term: "inference dimensionality"
    definition: "How much an agent has to infer, counted in the hidden variables and channels its model tracks. The ladder climbs by it."
  - term: "Pearl blanket"
    definition: "A Markov blanket in Pearl's sense. It is the set of variables that screens one part of a stated graph from the rest, and it is a fact about that graph."
  - term: "Friston blanket"
    definition: "A stronger reading from the free energy principle, in which a blanket is a physical boundary whose inside models the outside. No result here claims one."
  - term: "flux interface"
    definition: "The boundary of a nested region. The enclosing world sees the region only through what crosses it."
  - term: "coordinator"
    definition: "An optional agent one level above a region's cells. It reads what crosses the region's flux interface and writes a slow field back into the region. It has its own factor graph and its own expected free energy. Without one, no agent acts at the region's level."
  - term: "slow field"
    definition: "A signal the coordinator writes into a region's medium. It changes slowly next to the cells' own signals, and every cell in the region reads it."
  - term: "spillover"
    definition: "In the three-channel world, release at the end of a graph leaf that also reaches nearby cells, whether or not an edge joins them."
  - term: "endocrine broadcast"
    definition: "In the three-channel world, a signal that reaches every cell, wherever it sits in the graph."
  - term: "graph cut"
    definition: "A boundary drawn through the synapse graph. It splits the cells by the edges between them."
  - term: "field region"
    definition: "A boundary drawn in space. It holds the cells that sit inside it in the field."
  - term: "CI test"
    definition: "A conditional-independence test. It checks whether a proposed boundary screens its inside from its outside, which a Markov blanket has to do."
  - term: "inference gap"
    definition: "How far an agent's belief sits from the exact posterior, measured as a KL divergence. It is one term of the p* decomposition."
  - term: "LG"
    definition: "Linear-Gaussian. Dynamics and observations are linear and the noise is Gaussian. The cells here are LG as a declared approximation."
